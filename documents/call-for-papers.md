Dear colleagues,

We are pleased to invite submissions to the **Second Workshop on Ethics for Research and Teaching in Natural Language Processing (Ethics4NLP-2026)**, a half-day workshop co-located with the **Swedish Language Technology Conference (SLTC-2026)** on **25 November 2026** at **Luleå University of Technology**.

This workshop continues the work initiated by **Ethics4NLP-2024**: [https://gu-clasp.github.io/language-and-perception/events/ethics-for-nlp/](https://gu-clasp.github.io/language-and-perception/events/ethics-for-nlp/)

The workshop is organised by the Gothenburg, Stockholm, Uppsala, and Umeå interest group in ethics for NLP.

AI tasks involving the modelling of human language, as well as other decision-making applications, have developed substantially over the last several years. While AI systems trained on large amounts of data have achieved remarkable performance, it remains difficult to directly inspect what such models have learned. Instead, we can only observe their behaviour and performance, which may be biased in one or more ways and may have broader societal and environmental implications.

As a result, developers, researchers, and teachers of language technology have identified a need to address ethical issues related to data collection, model training, and the deployment of AI systems in real-world applications. Of particular importance are questions concerning the practical and ethical appropriateness of using AI in contexts that affect individuals and communities, as well as issues of cultural representation and impact. Given that many language models are developed by commercial companies outside Sweden, it is important that researchers, teachers, and students develop a deeper understanding of the implications of these technologies and promote ethically aware research and responsible use.

The workshop aims to bring together researchers, university teachers, Master's and PhD students, as well as partners from industry and the public sector who engage with ethical questions related to language technology and related disciplines, including computer vision, social robotics, machine learning, philosophy, medicine, law, media, and politics. We envision an interactive workshop with ample time for discussion, complemented by invited talks and presentations of ongoing or completed work.

## Topics of Interest

We welcome submissions on, but not limited to, the following topics:

* Biases in training data
* Biases in computational models
* Personal integrity, anonymisation, and pseudonymisation of data
* Intellectual property rights
* The use of language technology tools to detect unethical language use
* The use of language technology and its impact in under-resourced domains and communities (e.g. minority languages) and across cultures
* Legal aspects of language technology
* Philosophical questions related to AI and ethics
* Other related topics

The purpose of the workshop is to discuss these issues from a research perspective and to strengthen connections between research, teaching, and the use of language technology within universities, companies, organisations, and society more broadly.

## Submission Format

We invite submissions of **extended abstracts of 2 to 4 pages** (excluding references), formatted using the ACL style files:

* ACL formatting instructions: [https://acl-org.github.io/ACLPUB/formatting.html](https://acl-org.github.io/ACLPUB/formatting.html)

Authors may include appendices of any length.

We particularly encourage submissions describing **teaching materials, datasets, tools, methodologies, and other resources** developed for teaching and research in this area that authors wish to share with the wider community.

Accepted extended abstracts will be made available on the conference website. Submissions may report work that has been published elsewhere. Reviews will be **single-blind** and conducted by the programme committee.
